Facebook Ads Profitability

Online fashion store | May 2024 | Power BI

The question

The store ran 175 Facebook ad campaigns and spent 394M VND. A ROAS of 7.67 looked healthy, but the owner still did not know whether the ads were making money, which products caused losses, or where to move the budget next month.

What I did

  • Used Design Thinking to define four user groups and two North Star metrics: ROAS vs Break-even and Ads Profit.
  • Cleaned four Excel sheets in Power Query and created campaign IDs to link ads with products.
  • Connected two fact tables with no shared key by using TREATAS on date and product pairs.
  • Wrote DAX rules that label each campaign as Growth Opportunity, Optimization Required, Cap & Monitor or Reduce / Pause.

Key findings

  • With a gross margin of about 10%, the ads needed a ROAS of 9.80 to break even, so they lost about 86M VND overall.
  • 26 products priced below cost used 31.3% of ad spend. Without this group, the remaining ads earned about 180M VND.
  • Budget can move from 44 loss-making remarketing campaigns to 28 profitable ones without increasing total spend.
  • A high click-through rate did not mean a profitable campaign: the correlation between CTR and profit was only 0.06.

Card Payment Performance

Aurora Bank | Jan 2022 to Oct 2024 | Power BI

The question

Each team tracked declined card payments in its own report, so the Head of Card Division had no shared view of how reliable payments were, what caused declines, and which issues to fix first.

What I did

  • Defined the audience, seven key questions and the North Star metric (Success Rate) with Design Thinking.
  • Grouped 109 merchant category codes into 14 categories in Power Query and built a star schema.
  • Created four rules to flag unusual transactions, including a peer comparison that finds amounts in the top 1% of similar payments.
  • Designed a four-page report that moves from the overall picture to a ranked review queue.

Key findings

  • 98.26% of payments succeeded, and the monthly decline rate stayed between 1.35% and 2.13% with no upward trend.
  • Three reasons explain 90% of declines, led by insufficient balance at 64.3%.
  • Online declines come mainly from mistyped card details. Without them, the online decline rate is lower than chip payments.
  • Of 2,135 flagged transactions, only 39 need review first. A simple $1,000 threshold would have missed all 9 urgent cases.

Merchant Churn Prediction

Subscription app for Shopify stores | 2026 | Python

The question

The Customer Success team can only call a small share of paying merchants each month. Which merchants are most likely to cancel next month, and why?

What I did

  • Built 22 features from finance, revenue and search data with SQL in DuckDB, using only information available at each monthly snapshot.
  • Split the data by time: trained on June and July, tested once on August, then scored September.
  • Compared four models with grouped cross-validation and tuned LightGBM with Optuna. Models were chosen by PR-AUC because churn is rare (about 2.5%).
  • Used SHAP to give each merchant its three main risk reasons.

Key findings

  • Calling the top 5% of merchants ranked by the model reaches churners 5.7 times more often than random calls: 58 of 205, instead of about 10.
  • Falling searches and revenue are the strongest warning signs. Churning merchants saw revenue drop 20.3% and searches drop 15.1% in their last month.
  • New merchants churn most often (14.6% in their first month), yet 71% of all churn still comes from merchants with more than 24 months of tenure.

E-commerce Analysis with SQL

Google Merchandise Store | Aug 2016 to Aug 2017 | BigQuery

The question

Where does the store's traffic come from, how well does each source convert, and where do shoppers drop off before buying?

What I did

  • Answered 10 questions on traffic, bounce rate, conversion, revenue, cross-selling and the product funnel.
  • Worked with nested Google Analytics session data using UNNEST, wildcard tables and CTEs.
  • Wrote a short insight for each result and business recommendations at the end.

Key findings

  • Google search brings almost as many visits as direct traffic but converts 2.7 times lower (0.92% vs 2.48%).
  • The biggest drop in the funnel is from viewing to adding to cart: 63–72% of viewed products are never added.
  • Desktop generated 96.4% of 2017 revenue, while YouTube traffic had the highest bounce rate among large sources.

Bicycle Manufacturer Analysis

Adventure Works Cycles | 2011 to 2014 | BigQuery

The question

Which products and territories drive sales, do customers come back, and is production in line with demand?

What I did

  • Answered 8 questions from the Sales, Production and Purchasing teams using 8 tables from the AdventureWorks 2019 database.
  • Used window functions (DENSE_RANK, LAG, FIRST_VALUE), self-joins and a monthly cohort analysis for customer retention.
  • Wrote recommendations for each department based on the results.

Key findings

  • Mountain, Road and Touring Bikes brought in about 85% of the 45M USD revenue in the last 12 months.
  • Few customers return: after the first month, retention stays below 5% in almost every 2014 cohort.
  • Frames were overproduced, with a median stock-to-sales ratio of 3.6, and 224 purchase orders worth 3.87M USD were still pending in 2014.

VietDist Sales Data Pipeline

Sales and distribution | Python, PostgreSQL, Power BI | In progress

The question

Sales, targets, returns and staff data were spread across separate files on Google Drive. How can they become one reliable source for sales reporting?

What I did

  • Built Python loaders that read the source files from Google Drive and load them into PostgreSQL.
  • Organized the database in three layers: raw data (Bronze), cleaned data (Silver) and a star schema (Gold).
  • Modeled fact tables for sales, returns and targets, plus marts for sales vs target and distributor performance.
  • Tracked employee history with SCD Type 2 and kept every version of the sales target plan.

Data quality work

  • Profiled each source before cleaning, starting with 119,101 sales lines across 21 columns.
  • Documented data issues, assumptions and a data dictionary so every cleaning decision can be traced.
